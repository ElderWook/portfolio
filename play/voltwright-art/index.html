The one idea

VoltWright looks like a real circuit board, lit.

Every functional color traces to a standard a player can carry to a bench, never a mood board. Two research anchors: the PCB net-roles are the Okabe-Ito colorblind-safe palette, and the power phases are the real NEC/IEC wire code. Toggle a color-vision simulation up top. The palettes are engineered to survive it.

03

Phase palette · the power domain

RESEARCH ANCHOR ② · NEC/IEC wire code · core/standards/phase-identification.md

The teachable subtlety that makes it authentic: the NEC mandates only a few colors, and the rest is convention. Neutral (white/grey), EGC/ground (green), and the 4-wire-delta high-leg (orange) are fixed. The phase colors are employer/AHJ convention. Above 600 V there is no code at all, so you identify by stripe-count.

04

Status & semantic rules

The colors don’t just look real, they mean what they mean in the field, so the game never trains a wrong reflex.

energized / ok
#3ee484
correct · closed breaker · trace-energize sweep
warning
#ff9500
caution · fault-pending
fault
#ff3b30
short · violation · ground-current alarm

Green = ground / safe

On any conductor, green always means EGC/bonded, never a generic “success” that could mislead at a live panel.

Red = hazard

Fault, energized-danger, and the facility rule: current in a ground conductor is always alarm-red.

Two codes, separated

Net-role in the PCB view, phase in the wiring view. Where they meet, the safety-semantic (phase/ground) code wins.

CVD-safe > gated

Both anchors are distinguishable, so a colorblind-check joins the art gate. Verify it with the toggle above.
